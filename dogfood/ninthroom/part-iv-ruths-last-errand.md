# The Ninth Room

## Part IV: Ruth's Last Errand

Ruth did not live at Vesper House. She lodged in Greyhaven and normally travelled one stop by train to Fallowmere before continuing across the causeway to work.

On the morning of October 15, she used the outward half of a return ticket to travel from Greyhaven to Fallowmere, then continued to Vesper House for work. The unused half was intended for her journey home and remained in her handbag after her death.

A porter at Fallowmere Station remembered Ruth entering the parcels office at approximately 8:20 that evening. She showed him a telegram delivered to her at Vesper House less than an hour earlier:

> **MISS DALLOW—COME TO THE PARCELS OFFICE AT 8:20. BRING YOUR CATALOGUE NOTES.**  
> **—A.B.**

A station messenger's receipt recorded delivery to `R. DALLOW — VESPER GATE` at 7:24. The signature on the receipt was handwritten. The signature `A.B.` on the telegram itself was typed.

Aldous had denied sending the message when Merritt questioned him on October 16.

The railway telegraph office kept a retained carbon of each transmitted form in addition to the copy delivered to its recipient. The stationmaster's retained copy matched the paper Ruth showed the porter. On both, the capital **M** sat slightly above the surrounding letters.

Merritt tested the Bell estate-office typewriter at Fallowmere. Its capital **M** rose by the same small amount. Felix used that machine for routine estate correspondence. Gideon used it for legal and financial business.

The office daybook for October 15 contained these entries:

> **6:32 — OFFICE KEY 2; private office use.**
>
> **6:57 — telegram form taken to station counter.**
>
> **7:06 — OFFICE KEY 2 returned.**

The telegraph counter stamped Ruth's summons at 6:59.

Felix's dated work diary placed Felix at Vesper House throughout that interval. Two earlier lines in the same diary read:

> **5:35 — R.D. comparing Row 17 restoration papers.**  
> **5:45 — G.R. at her desk; asks what she has found. Notebook open.**

Neither Felix nor Gideon would identify office key 2 from memory. Felix said the numbered keys were sometimes exchanged when one man ran an errand for the other. The master assignment register was kept with closed files at Gideon's chambers and had not yet reached Vesper House.

Gideon acknowledged speaking with Ruth at her desk earlier that evening. He said he could not remember noticing her notebook.

The porter heard Ruth arguing with a man in the foggy freight yard shortly before her death. He could not identify the voice. The man's words were too low to distinguish, but Ruth's reply carried clearly through the fog:

> “Account? I know of no account. You have mistaken the number. I was examining the row, not the account.”

The porter heard hurried footsteps and the clang of freight couplings soon afterward. By the time he reached the yard, the fog concealed anyone moving away.

Ruth's body was found beside a freight siding behind the parcels office, not beside a passenger platform. Wagons were being moved slowly back and forth there to assemble a train.

Ruth was severely short-sighted. Her spectacles were folded in her handbag.

Among her effects was a catalogue notebook. Its last page had been torn out. Impressions remained on the page beneath:

> **ROW 17**  
> **BLUE FIBRES IN PAPER**  
> **NOT CONTEMPORARY**  
> **ASK B.O. BEFORE TELLING A.B.**

Row 17 of the Vesper collection contained the **Vesper Psalter**, supposedly produced in the fifteenth century.

Miriam examined an exposed paper edge with a laboratory lens. She reported seeing blue fibers of a kind ordinarily associated with modern machine-made paper.

Beatrice objected to opening the Psalter.

> “Modern repair paper was introduced during an earlier restoration,” she said. “Ruth knew that perfectly well.”

Merritt asked why Ruth had written `NOT CONTEMPORARY` and intended to ask `B.O.` before telling `A.B.`

Beatrice looked at the closed Psalter.

> “I cannot answer for a dead woman's choice of words.”
