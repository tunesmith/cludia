# The Ninth Room

## Part II: The Brass Clasp

Dr. Pike examined Aldous while the others remained downstairs.

She reported that the puncture on his thumb was recent. The blue-black color surrounding it was not bruising but a foreign marking dye. His posture and pupils were consistent with aconitine poisoning, although she could not prove that diagnosis without chemical analysis.

According to Pike, aconitine introduced through a puncture might leave a victim capable of speaking for several minutes. Death could follow ten to thirty minutes later through disruption of the heart.

She also disclosed that Aldous had long suffered from an irregular heartbeat.

> “A severe attack could imitate some signs of poisoning,” she said. “It would not explain the puncture or the dye.”

Mrs. Wren supplied another fact about the clock. At approximately 6:12 that evening, she had noticed that it showed exactly six.

> “I offered to correct it,” she said. “Mr. Bell told me to leave it alone.”

Beatrice examined the book without touching its clasp. She explained that early printed sheets were folded into gatherings. Their outer folds sometimes remained joined until a reader slit them. A genuinely unopened seventeenth-century copy could be valuable precisely because no owner had read beyond those folds.

> “Aldous would inspect the paper, type, sewing, and binding before opening a single additional leaf,” she said.

The first sixteen leaves formed two old octavo gatherings. Everything beyond their boundary belonged to a different block. The later paper looked newer, and the spine was unusually thick. Felix recalled that the parcel had surprised Aldous by its weight.

Cassian studied the clasp with a magnifying lens. On its inner face he found a narrow groove stained with the same blue-black substance present on Aldous's thumb. The groove was too precisely machined to be seventeenth-century work. Something slender had occupied it, but it was empty.

When Cassian pressed the clasp shut with the handle of a spoon, it resisted briefly and then snapped closed with disproportionate force. His expression changed.

> “A spring catch,” he said. “Beyond that, I should not guess.”

Merritt asked whether he had seen another fitting behave in the same way.

> “Not this fitting,” Cassian said.

He would say no more.

No one permitted him to dismantle it before a professional examination.

The parcel had been delivered at 4:00 by a Fallowmere carrier. Its wrapper bore the printed label of **March & Rowe, Antiquarian Booksellers**.

Gideon said the firm had ceased trading five years earlier.

Beatrice replied that Aldous had purchased a bundle of its old labels when the premises were cleared. The bundle was stored with packing materials in the Bell estate office at Fallowmere. Felix and Gideon used that office regularly; Beatrice sometimes collected old labels there for museum shipments.

Felix had accepted the parcel at Vesper House. There was no invoice, sender's address, or covering letter.

He recalled Aldous weighing it in his hands while it was still wrapped.

> “There is something inside this book which the sender does not expect me to read,” Aldous had said.

Merritt asked how Aldous could have known that the parcel contained a book.

Felix turned over the wrapper. In one corner, almost hidden beneath the obsolete bookseller's label, was a typed description:

> **ONE VELLUM VOLUME — TIDAL DISSERTATION**

The capital **M** in `VOLUME` sat slightly above the other letters.

Felix had brought three estate-office registers to Vesper House that afternoon for Aldous's quarterly review. Before setting them aside for the night, Merritt copied several entries. He read them aloud without comment.

From the packing-material register:

> **MARCH & ROWE LABELS — BUNDLE OF 46**  
> Routine users: F.H.; G.R.  
> Museum withdrawals: B.O.

From an instruction memorandum in Aldous's hand:

> **JUNE SECURITY REVIEW — G.R. TO ASSIST**  
> Signature stamps; duplicate and spare keys; chemical stores; cabinet locks; alarm fittings; safe and strong-room miscellany.

And from the chemical-stock index:

> **30 SEPTEMBER — CABINET INVENTORY VERIFIED**  
> Initials: *M.B.*

Mrs. Wren glanced at the clock on the mantelpiece downstairs.

It was then eleven minutes past midnight.
