# The Ninth Room — Epilogue and Solution

> **Spoilers:** Do not provide this document until the solver has finished Part VI and made an accusation or requested the solution.

## The accusation

**Gideon Rusk killed both Ruth Dallow and Aldous Bell.**

The mystery has three revelations, but they do not all belong to the same crime:

- The false object was Beatrice Orr's facsimile of the Vesper Psalter.
- The false account was Escrow 17, through which Gideon embezzled from the Bell estate.
- Gideon killed Ruth to protect that embezzlement, then killed Aldous to prevent its exposure.

The gray vellum tide book was also a deceptive object, but it was not the object *in Aldous's collection* to which his dinner announcement referred. At that point it was still an unopened delivery.

## Why Ruth died

Ruth was investigating the object in **Row 17**, not the financial account called **Escrow 17**. Her notebook concerned blue fibers in the Vesper Psalter's paper and Beatrice's explanation for them.

Before Ruth died, the only people shown to know that the private account was numbered seventeen were Aldous and Gideon. The account agreement named them, and its statements travelled under seal between Gideon's chambers and Aldous's private ledger. Felix handled ordinary estate bookkeeping but did not know the account number.

The man in the freight yard believed Ruth's number referred to an account. Her protest therefore carries more information than the porter understood:

> "You have mistaken the number. I was examining the row, not the account."

Aldous was at Vesper House with Beatrice and Dr. Pike when Ruth died. Gideon was the other person with the necessary knowledge.

The estate-office records supply the rest. Office key 2 was used shortly before the false message was filed; the master register later identified that key as Gideon's. He signed the message `A.B.` so Ruth would obey, summoned her to a secluded railway office, and told her to bring the notes he feared.

At the meeting, Gideon learned that Ruth had not discovered Escrow 17. By demanding an account explanation she did not understand, however, he revealed that a concealed account existed. If Ruth returned to Aldous and repeated the conversation, the fraud would be uncovered. Gideon struggled with her and pushed her into the path of the slowly shunted freight wagons.

Her unused return ticket, folded spectacles, location away from the passenger platform, and pre-impact bruising collectively refute an intended death or an ordinary misstep.

## How Aldous solved Ruth's death

After Merritt showed him the notebook impressions and repeated the porter's account, Aldous compared **Row 17** with **Escrow 17**. He inspected the account, found stamped authorizations and payments to a defunct bookseller, and demanded the complete vouchers from Gideon.

His unfinished sentence would have continued approximately:

> "The mistake was assuming that Ruth had discovered the account."

The burned hearth fragment came from the missing Escrow 17 statement. Its edge fitted the scorched strip in the ledger. The letters `ESC` had burned away, leaving `ROW—17` visible. The surviving substring initially pointed toward the collection row even though the document came from the account.

The statement travelled through Gideon's chambers and returned already scorched the day after Aldous demanded the vouchers. That custody, timing, and Gideon's motive support the conclusion that he damaged it while trying to conceal the account, although the murder case does not depend on proving who applied the flame. Aldous later removed the loose fragment while examining the ledger and carried it to the Ninth Room with his other evidence. It fell against the cold fender during his collapse. The hearth itself had not burned it.

## Why the murder was planned before dinner

Gideon did not invent Aldous's murder after hearing the theatrical dinner announcement.

On October 16, Aldous had already demanded every Escrow 17 voucher and warned Gideon that the discrepancies would be exposed on October 18. The bookbinder's examination placed assembly of the mechanism no later than October 17. Gideon therefore had both warning and time to prepare it.

He possessed overlapping forms of access that were separately shared with other suspects:

- Miriam controlled the current poison-cabinet key, but the security annex assigned the duplicate to the holder of office key 2—Gideon.
- Cassian designed the Thorn, while Aldous, Gideon, and Felix had all handled copies of its plans.
- Felix commonly used the estate-office typewriter, but Gideon had the documented private office periods immediately before Ruth's message and Aldous's parcel.
- Felix and Gideon could use Aldous's signature stamp, but only Gideon administered the secret account and received its statements.
- Beatrice had access to March & Rowe labels, but so did Felix and Gideon.

No single capability identifies Gideon. Their intersection does.

## The poisoned parcel

Gideon used the retained duplicate key to take the aconitine after Miriam's September 30 inventory. Working from the Thorn plans, he fitted a spring-driven needle into the brass clasp of a recently rebound book.

The old opening leaves supplied a plausible title and imprint. The remaining modern, uncut paper supplied bulk around a hollow spine. The heavy construction prompted Aldous's remark that the sender had placed something inside the book that was not meant to be read.

On October 18, office key 2 was used at the Fallowmere estate office. The recipient's address was typed on its defective machine, and the carrier collected the parcel minutes later. The master register identified Gideon as the assigned holder of key 2; Felix was documented at Vesper House. An old March & Rowe label made the parcel resemble a legitimate antiquarian delivery while also exploiting a name already familiar in Bell estate business.

## The locked room

The locked room was genuine but irrelevant to the method.

Aldous took the parcel into the Ninth Room and locked himself in. His voice at 9:18 was genuine. He had not yet succumbed to poison.

When he examined the brass clasp, the needle projected through its groove and punctured the base of his thumb. Releasing the clasp withdrew the needle into the hollow spine. This explains all four physical observations:

- the puncture on Aldous's thumb;
- matching blue-black dye on the thumb, groove, and needle;
- the apparently empty groove;
- and the absence of a visible weapon.

Aldous never began reading. His spectacles remained folded and the modern leaves remained unopened.

The aconitine acted after a delay. At approximately 9:39, Aldous collapsed, driving his chair into the desk. The impact bent the clock's balance staff and stopped it. Because the clock was twelve minutes slow, its face displayed 9:27. Mrs. Wren heard the chair scrape at approximately the same moment.

Merritt's uninterrupted view of the billiard-room door establishes that Gideon was playing billiards when Aldous collapsed. The alibi is true. It covers the poison's delayed effect, not the earlier theft, construction, typing, and delivery of the mechanism.

## Why the other suspects fit individual clues

- **Miriam** had chemical expertise and the current cabinet key, but lacked the plan and financial motive.
- **Felix** handled the parcel, used the typewriter and stamp, had seen Aldous's plan copy, and concealed a real reason to fear the quarterly review. His unauthorized loan guarantee and secret engagement made him a serious alternative for Aldous's murder. Ruth's letter and the repayment receipt explain that secrecy; the numbered-key records place him at Vesper while key 2 was used to prepare the summons and parcel, and he lacked prior knowledge of Escrow 17's number.
- **Dr. Pike** knew Aldous's heart condition and understood aconitine, but lacked the plans, packing opportunity, and account motive.
- **Cassian** designed the mechanism and possessed the greatest mechanical skill, but lacked access to the poison and had no connection to Escrow 17.
- **Beatrice** forged the Psalter and lied about Ruth's discovery, but had a corroborated alibi for Ruth's death and lacked the combined means for Aldous's murder.
- **Gideon** alone possessed the account knowledge and motive, controlled office key 2 and the duplicate poison key assigned to it, possessed the plans, had the two relevant estate-office preparation windows, and benefited from a genuine alibi at the delayed time of death.

## The intended proof

The solution is not “the clue belonging to Gideon.” It is the intersection:

1. Ruth's killer knew about an account numbered seventeen before Ruth did.
2. Gideon and Aldous alone had that demonstrated knowledge; Aldous had an alibi and later investigated her death.
3. Gideon had motive to recover Ruth's notes and silence her.
4. The numbered-key records identify Gideon as the office user who had the preparation opportunity for the false summons.
5. Aldous's investigation threatened the same concealed account.
6. Gideon retained the duplicate poison key and possessed the mechanical plans.
7. The same numbered-key custody chain gave Gideon poison access and the documented opportunity to prepare the parcel before delivery, while Felix remained at Vesper House.
8. The book mechanism killed after a delay, making Gideon's verified billiards alibi compatible with guilt.

The false Psalter explains Ruth's real investigation. Escrow 17 explains Gideon's fatal misunderstanding. The poisoned book explains the locked room. The clock and chair establish when the delayed mechanism finished its work.
